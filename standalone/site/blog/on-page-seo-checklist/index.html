On-Page SEO Checklist: Pages, Intent, Links, and QA

A practical checklist that avoids keyword stuffing and duplicate landing pages.

Practical guide · Evaluation criteria, examples and next steps

A page update needs a single job: answer a buyer question, present accurate proof and make the next step obvious. Metadata changes alone will not rescue the wrong page.

Assign the page a real job

Before editing a title tag, identify the visitor's question and the action the page should support. A service page should explain the offer and how to engage. A guide should teach the subject. A comparison should establish criteria. When multiple pages serve the same intent, consolidation may be more useful than new copy.

  • Primary reader and intent
  • Page purpose and call to action
  • Existing competing URLs

Write a specific title and first screen

Use a title that accurately describes the content and distinguishes the page from nearby URLs. The H1 and introduction should match the promise without turning into a list of keyword variants. Avoid unsupported superlatives and labels that do not reflect the business.

  • Clear and unique title
  • Accurate H1 and supporting introduction
  • Helpful above-the-fold context

Improve supporting content

Address buyer questions, include product or service limitations, and use real evidence. Break long explanations into meaningful headings, lists, and examples. For specialist topics, attach sources and obtain appropriate reviewer approval.

  • Subheadings for distinct questions
  • Relevant proof and caveats
  • Accessible media with descriptive alt text

Connect the page to the rest of the site

Relevant internal links help users explore related services and research. Choose anchor text that describes the destination naturally. Review broken links and ensure pages are discoverable from main hubs or related content.

  • Service-to-guide links
  • Contextual related articles
  • Breadcrumbs and navigation

Validate the published change

After approval, check the live URL, metadata, canonical, heading structure, links, structured data where applicable, and mobile presentation. Use Search Console and analytics over time to understand whether the page is reaching the intended queries and visitors.

  • Live-page QA
  • Indexing and canonical status
  • Outcome measurement with dates

Worked example: one service page, not five keyword variants

Imagine a commercial page for technical SEO audits. A useful improvement plan is to align the title, H1, service explanation, internal links and contact action around the same buyer intent rather than multiplying near-duplicate pages.

Title and primary headingExplain the actual service and audience clearlyDo the title/H1 agree about the main offer?
Intro and proofAnswer who it is for, deliverables and review processCan a buyer tell what they receive within one screen?
Internal linksLink to relevant audit sample, pricing and contactAre links descriptive and useful in context?

The checklist is not a request to repeat one phrase a set number of times. Relevance comes from a useful answer to the intended search and a page that works.

Frequently asked questions

Should I repeat the exact keyword in every heading?

No. Use clear language and natural related terms where they help the reader.

Can on-page SEO fix a site with technical problems?

Sometimes it helps, but crawl/indexing and other technical issues may need separate work.

Should I create location variants of each service page?

Only when they serve different legitimate user needs and provide meaningful local information.
