SEO Agency Profit Margins: How Outsourcing Changes the Math

Include briefing, reviews, software and revisions when estimating an account’s delivery cost.

Practical guide · Evaluation criteria, examples and next steps

Calculate delivery cost after account management, reviews and rework. A cheaper vendor does not improve margin if your team spends the savings correcting every deliverable.

Calculate contribution margin at the account level

Start with what a client actually pays, then subtract direct labor, vendor delivery, required tools, account-specific reporting, and other variable costs. Do not confuse revenue with profit or use only the vendor invoice as the cost of service. Agency overhead and sales acquisition costs matter in broader profitability analysis.

  • Net client revenue
  • Direct delivery and review hours
  • Tools and third-party expenses

Include your internal supervision cost

An outsourced task that needs repeated rewrites may be more expensive than it appears. Record the hours spent preparing briefs, clarifying requirements, reviewing output, and correcting errors. A reliable brief and acceptance checklist can improve margin by reducing this hidden work.

  • Briefing and project management time
  • First-pass acceptance rate
  • Revision and escalation time

Use scope to prevent margin leakage

Fixed monthly retainers can become unprofitable when new requests are added without tradeoffs. Define the execution queue, acceptance conditions, reviewer, and policy for out-of-scope work. Show clients how priorities change when they add requests rather than making silent promises.

  • Priority queue and capacity guardrails
  • Change-request approval
  • Client dependency log

Choose the right model for workload variability

Project-based outsourcing is useful for irregular work. Recurring support can create consistency when a predictable set of accounts needs updates every month. Dedicated capacity may fit agencies with sufficient volume and a mature task process. The cheapest model per task is not necessarily cheapest overall.

  • Workload consistency
  • Coordination burden
  • Quality requirements

Measure healthy growth, not just bigger retainers

Track account contribution, capacity utilization, on-time acceptance, and customer satisfaction using defined methods. Profit improvement should not come from skimping on quality, misrepresenting work, or offering guarantees the agency cannot meet.

  • Contribution after direct costs
  • Accepted output per planned service
  • Scope disputes and churn indicators

Example: estimate contribution margin before reselling SEO

Illustrative scenario: an agency charges $2,000/month for an SEO account, pays $850 for scoped outsourced delivery, spends $250 on account management and $100 on tools and review. These are example assumptions, not market benchmarks.

Example or checkpointRecommended actionReview or acceptance
Client revenue$2,000 per monthAmount billed before taxes and any pass-through adjustments
Direct delivery and support$850 + $250 + $100 = $1,200Include real supervisory effort and approved third-party cost
Contribution$2,000 − $1,200 = $800Amount before shared overhead and tax
Contribution margin$800 ÷ $2,000 = 40%Not the agency’s net profit margin

Before deciding whether to resell a package, test a downside case: a difficult account adds five hours of review or requires unexpected development. If that turns the contribution negative, change the scope or pricing instead of hiding the work.

Include the cost of correction

Contribution margin is a useful starting point for agency pricing. But two vendors with the same monthly fee can produce very different outcomes when one needs multiple rounds of account-manager rework. The calculator below is an illustrative planning tool; it does not use actual company finances.

  1. Count the vendor invoice and the internal hours spent on briefing, review and client communication.
  2. Treat unexpected changes, revisions and tool fees as delivery costs rather than “free” time.
  3. Use a realistic range of workload, not only the easiest month.
  4. Compare the projected margin with the agency’s minimum acceptable level.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good SEO agency margin?

There is no single safe target for every model. Compare your own fully loaded costs, service mix, client commitments, and overhead.

Can outsourcing improve profit margins?

It can reduce fixed hiring needs or improve specialization, but management, QA, and rework costs must be included.

Should I sell SEO packages by hours or outcomes?

Align pricing to defined scope, effort, and business value while avoiding guarantees of search outcomes.
