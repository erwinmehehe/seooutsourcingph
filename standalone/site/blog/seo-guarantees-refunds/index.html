SEO Money-Back Guarantees and Refund Policies: What to Check

How to distinguish a meaningful deliverable commitment from an unrealistic ranking promise.

Practical guide · Evaluation criteria, examples and next steps

A useful service guarantee addresses work the supplier controls: a missed handoff, a deficient report or a failure to meet agreed acceptance criteria. It cannot make search engines behave on schedule.

Separate controlled work from uncontrolled outcomes

An SEO company can commit to the tasks it performs and the standards it uses. It cannot control search ranking systems, competitor changes, or every implementation dependency. A claim that guarantees a specific organic ranking or lead count deserves careful scrutiny.

  • Deliverable commitment
  • Review and acceptance standard
  • External dependencies

Read the guarantee wording carefully

Some service agreements define remediation if an agreed deliverable is late or incomplete. Others use promotional language about rankings that cannot be verified fairly. Ask what qualifies, what evidence is required, how long the remedy lasts, and what costs are excluded.

  • Exactly what is guaranteed
  • How disputes and fixes are handled
  • Fees and third-party expenses excluded

Check the measurement definition

A ranking promise without the search engine, location, device, query set, and date is ambiguous. Even with these details, positions change and do not necessarily produce qualified customers. Good evaluation includes accepted work and transparent measurement of observations.

  • Consistent keyword and market settings
  • Source URLs and dates
  • Commercial outcome caveats

Use a well-scoped first project

A pilot can test whether the team produces accurate briefs, fixes, reports, and communication. A written acceptance checklist is more useful than a broad promise that 'SEO will work.' If work misses the agreed criteria, the contract should define review, correction, and escalation.

  • Acceptance criteria
  • Revision and correction process
  • Ownership of assets and access

Get independent contract advice when needed

This article is practical buying information, not legal advice. Refund rights and advertising restrictions differ by location and contract terms. For important engagements, have the agreement reviewed by a qualified professional before relying on any promotional assurance.

  • Scope and change-control terms
  • Jurisdiction and dispute process
  • Documented deliverable records

Separate deliverable remediation from ranking promises

A client contract can define what happens if an agreed deliverable is late or fails acceptance. That is different from promising a #1 ranking, a specific number of leads or a refund triggered by an external algorithm.

Example or checkpointRecommended actionReview or acceptance
Deliverable acceptanceList output, deadline, reviewer and objective checksA failed check can trigger rework within the contract
Scope changeDocument additional pages, tools and approvalsAgree price/timeline impact before executing
Performance reportingDefine data sources, location and measurement windowDo not equate movement with guaranteed business results
Refund or credit termsDescribe lawful conditions, limits and excluded feesConfirm the service agreement before work starts

Frequently asked questions

Can any agency guarantee #1 organic rankings?

No provider controls every ranking factor or search result.

Is a deliverable-remediation clause different?

Yes. A clause about correcting work within an agreed scope is different from guaranteeing rankings or revenue.

Does every SEO contract require a refund policy?

Applicable requirements depend on the business, jurisdiction, and contract; obtain professional advice as needed.
