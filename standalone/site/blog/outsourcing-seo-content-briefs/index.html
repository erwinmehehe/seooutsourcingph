How to Brief Outsourced SEO Content Writers

The source inputs and review gates that prevent generic content and keep each SEO article accurate, useful, and on-brand.

Practical guide · Evaluation criteria, examples and next steps

The most valuable line in a content brief is often the source material the writer must use. Without it, a polished draft can still be wrong.

Define the intended reader and decision

A brief should describe who needs the article, what question they want answered, and what the organization can credibly provide. A keyword alone is too vague. Specify whether the page should help a reader compare providers, understand a process, or take the next commercial step.

  • Audience and search intent
  • Page owner and content goal
  • Commercial or informational next step

Supply factual source material

Writers cannot safely invent product features, service areas, patient outcomes, client testimonials, or compliance statements. Provide approved website pages, reference documents, terminology rules, and a contact for unanswered questions. Distinguish facts the writer may state from claims needing review.

  • Approved service details
  • Evidence and references
  • Claims requiring specialist approval

Map the page into the site

The brief should identify the canonical target URL, related service pages, internal-link sources, and likely overlapping posts. If the topic already has a strong page, consider refreshing it before commissioning a new article.

  • Parent topic and page ownership
  • Inbound and outbound internal links
  • Cannibalization and duplicate checks

Specify the output format and quality bar

Define headings as decision topics, not arbitrary keyword containers. Require source notes, metadata suggestions, alt-text guidance, CTA placement, and a checklist for readability and accuracy. Explain what will cause a draft to be rejected so revisions are predictable.

  • Outline and required sections
  • Editorial QA checklist
  • Handoff format and approval stage

Turn revisions into better systems

When the same edits occur repeatedly, update the writing brief, examples, and approval rules. Track first-pass acceptance rather than only articles delivered. That is how outsourced content improves with experience rather than producing the same generic mistakes each month.

  • Revision patterns
  • Accepted examples
  • Updated style and source rules

Annotated content brief: comparison page

A brief should make the writer’s choices reviewable. Consider a comparison article for two service options rather than a list of keywords and an arbitrary word count.

Example or checkpointRecommended actionReview or acceptance
Reader and intentA buyer deciding which service fits a particular situationState a decision question that the finished page must answer
Required evidenceProduct/service facts, limitations, dated primary sourcesEach material claim has a source and reviewer
Recommended structureDefinitions, decision factors, tradeoffs, next stepsH2s follow reader questions rather than keyword variations
Acceptance criteriaAccuracy, originality, links, accessible formattingEditor signs off before CMS publication

An approved brief does not automatically authorize publication. The business remains the final source of truth on service scope, costs, and regulated claims.

A brief should identify what not to write

The most important field in a high-risk SEO brief may be the list of unsupported claims. Document unknown fees, clinical claims, product specifications and warranties that the writer must leave for approval instead of filling gaps with plausible prose.

  1. Approved sources: links or named stakeholders for facts and numbers.
  2. Page job: the buyer decision and single conversion action.
  3. Prohibited assumptions: data, claims or guarantees not established by sources.
  4. Acceptance: review for factual accuracy, distinct value and fit with the rest of the site.

Download: Content acceptance sheet (CSV). Use the example to guide a review; no campaign outcome is implied.

Frequently asked questions

Can writers publish directly?

Only if authorized and after any required factual, brand, legal, and quality review.

Should every blog be 1,000 words?

No. Useful depth depends on the topic and reader need, not a fixed word count.

How do we protect brand voice?

Supply examples, terminology, do-not-say rules, and an accountable editor.
