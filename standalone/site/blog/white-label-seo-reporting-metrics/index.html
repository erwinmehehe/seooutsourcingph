What SEO Metrics Belong in a White-Label Client Report?

Metrics, context, and narrative for reports your agency can confidently send.

Practical guide · Evaluation criteria, examples and next steps

An agency report must separate what shipped, what search performance did and which decisions remain open. Mixing those three stories makes the report impossible to act on.

Start with what the client is trying to achieve

A local service client cares about qualified enquiries, while a publisher may prioritize engaged organic visits. Reporting should choose a small set of decision-useful measures rather than include every available chart. Define the source and conversion meaning before the first report.

  • Business goal and measurement period
  • Primary conversions and attribution limitations
  • Priority pages and services

Use rankings as diagnostic evidence

Keyword positions should include location, device, tool, search engine, and date. Show distribution across position bands and changes for agreed keywords, not a handpicked list of only winners. Group brand terms separately where possible.

  • Tracked keyword coverage
  • Positions by segment
  • Queries newly visible or declining

Connect search performance to landing pages

Use Search Console impressions and clicks with filters that a reviewer can reproduce. Review landing pages and conversion paths in analytics, remembering that GSC and analytics measure different things. Explain major tracking gaps instead of silently treating the numbers as precise.

  • Non-branded organic impressions and clicks
  • Engaged landing-page visits
  • Calls, forms, demos, or bookings where reliable

Report delivery evidence

Clients deserve to see what work was completed. Link to approved pages, release notes, issue records, content briefs, and profile changes. Separate proposed, approved, live, verified, and blocked statuses. This reduces the temptation to equate activity count with impact.

  • Completed deliverables
  • Work waiting on approval
  • Next cycle priorities with owner

End with a decision

A good report states what changed, what is uncertain, and what should happen next. If a page gained impressions but not qualified leads, the next step might be a conversion review. If indexing remains blocked, the next step is technical implementation, not more blog posts.

  • Interpretation of movement
  • Risks and dependencies
  • Actions for the next review

Example monthly report with decision context

Separate delivered work from observed search metrics. If rankings move but the content release is still blocked, a client should be able to see both facts without implying a causal link.

Example or checkpointRecommended actionReview or acceptance
Completed SEO workList accepted tasks and release referencesWhich deliverables were checked and deployed?
Search visibilityReport date range, non-brand filter, location/device and sourceWhich changes are observations, and where is uncertainty?
Business actionsRecord leads or events with event definitions and attribution caveatsDid qualified enquiries change? What data are missing?
Blocked and nextName approval, access or implementation dependenciesWho makes the next decision and by when?

A good white-label summary can be read by both the client-facing account manager and a technical reviewer. It should never hide missing conversions or silently change keyword sets to improve the story.

Lead with the decision the report should support

An agency report that leads with “keyword rankings improved” may conceal that the tracked queries have little commercial intent. Write a one-paragraph decision summary first, then show the numbers needed to support or challenge it.

  1. Separate branded and non-branded query changes and keep the filters visible.
  2. Explain affected landing pages and work completed, not only aggregate traffic.
  3. List unapproved or blocked tasks so the account manager can address dependencies.
  4. Record the next month’s decision and owner.

Frequently asked questions

Should rankings or traffic come first?

Choose the reporting order based on the client objective, but show the relationship between visibility and useful business outcomes.

Can a report be fully white-label?

Yes, under agreed brand and approval rules; raw data sources should still be traceable internally.

Is estimated traffic the same as actual visits?

No. Clearly label third-party estimates and keep them separate from analytics measurements.

A report should separate three different questions

Avoid mixing output metrics, search measurements and business outcomes. A clear report explains the difference before it describes the trend.
