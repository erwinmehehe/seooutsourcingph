How to Choose a White-Label SEO Partner in the Philippines

An evidence-based vendor scorecard for agencies handing over client-facing work.

Practical guide · Evaluation criteria, examples and next steps

Have each candidate complete the same small work brief and return an issue log, approval notes and a finished handoff. Compare the work, not the pitch.

Separate commercial ownership from delivery

A white-label partnership begins with explicit boundaries. Your agency usually owns the contract, client relationship, strategy promises, and final presentation. The partner owns only assigned work and agreed internal communication. Put those rules in writing, including what happens when a client asks for urgent changes or the vendor identifies a risk outside scope.

  • Client contact rules
  • Change approval rights
  • Ownership of working files and final outputs

Inspect work, not just case-study headlines

Ask for a redacted technical ticket, a complete content brief, and a monthly decision report. Look for evidence, named review criteria, and realistic limitations. Screenshots of ranking positions are useful context, but do not validate the quality of a particular provider without supporting information about the engagement.

  • Findings trace back to real URLs or approved examples.
  • Reports distinguish shipped work from work waiting on approvals.
  • Recommendations can be independently checked.

Run a paid pilot

Choose a task representative of the account types you intend to delegate. A narrow technical audit or five-page optimization batch can expose misunderstanding, communication delays, and QA gaps. Ask both teams to log revisions, handoff time, and blockers so the decision to expand is based on observed performance.

  • Measure first-pass acceptance.
  • Check response and escalation quality.
  • Validate tool permissions and security handling.

Price for delivery and supervision

The vendor rate is one part of the agency's margin. Include your account management, strategy, QA, revisions, reporting, tools, and client support. A cheap task with frequent rework can cost more than a slightly higher-priced service that arrives accepted and ready for a client.

  • Compare all-in delivery cost.
  • Define scope-change rules.
  • Avoid selling output quantities the vendor has not committed to.

Formalize the operating rules

Once the pilot passes, document the task template, status labels, owner, reviewer, response expectations, acceptance criteria, and reporting dates. Avoid scattered instructions across chat, email, and files. One source of truth makes white-label delivery easier to scale and easier to audit.

  • One task system and artifact repository.
  • Named reviewer and escalation contact.
  • A recurring improvement review.

Vendor evaluation scorecard you can reuse

Score three candidate partners on observable delivery evidence, not sales claims. A pilot should include a representative brief, a test handoff, and a retrospective. Ratings below are an illustrative evaluation framework, not an assessment of any real agency.

Example or checkpointRecommended actionReview or acceptance
Sample quality0 = examples unavailable; 1 = template only; 2 = complete redacted sample with evidenceAsk who checks source accuracy before client delivery
QA and revision rules0 = informal; 1 = limited SOP; 2 = written acceptance checklist and rework procedureName the final reviewer on both sides
Access and confidentiality0 = shared passwords; 1 = partial controls; 2 = least-privilege accounts and documented boundariesConfirm no client contact without consent
Delivery reliability0 = no issue log; 1 = status reports; 2 = documented blockers, owners and response processTest a missed dependency in the pilot

A partner scoring eight points in this sample framework is not automatically a good fit. Review the high-risk categories individually: weak security or unverifiable work should be addressed before expansion even if the total looks acceptable.

Ask to see the work that failed QA

Agencies often examine a supplier’s best screenshot. The more revealing question is how the supplier responds when a brief is incomplete, a sensitive claim is unsupported, or the reviewer rejects an output. Request an anonymized example of a correction loop, with confidential details removed.

  1. Was the problem identified before client delivery?
  2. Was the rejected claim or missing evidence documented?
  3. Did the revised artifact reference the original task and preserve version history?
  4. Could the agency reproduce the decision without speaking to the original worker?

Frequently asked questions

Should an agency ask for a non-disclosure agreement?

Confidentiality and data-handling terms are common considerations. Have the agreement reviewed for the business and jurisdiction.

Can a white-label partner write under our brand?

Yes, when the service scope, voice rules, approvals, and attribution responsibilities are agreed.

Should we rely on the cheapest provider?

No. Evaluate total delivery cost, quality, supervision, and repeatability.

Run an evaluation call with evidence, not slogans

Ask each shortlisted vendor to demonstrate the same anonymized sample: a brief, a delivered ticket, a QA response and a revised handoff. Score clarity, ownership and traceability separately.

Evaluation elementPractical check
Show one acceptance criterionWhat would make you reject this deliverable?
Explain a missed dependencyWho flags it and how quickly is the agency informed?
Describe access removalHow are credentials revoked when an account ends?
Provide a sample weekly statusCan the manager identify work needing a decision in under two minutes?
