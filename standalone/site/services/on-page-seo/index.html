Improve a page’s job before its keywords

On-page optimization is not a contest to repeat a keyword more times. A useful page should answer the visitor, signal what the service offers, link to related content, and give a clear next action. Changes must fit the brand and the business facts.

A site may have many pages that are technically indexable but poorly aligned with the queries they attract. Common issues include competing pages with nearly identical intent, titles promising services the page does not discuss, missing internal links, and headings that hide the actual offer.

Intent, facts and internal linking

We map the target intent to the strongest existing URL, inspect competing query types, and determine whether the page needs a refresh, consolidation, or a new supporting page. A revision brief records the current state, proposed headings, metadata, internal links, FAQs, and approval requirements.

The standard for completion

Check the published page against the approved brief: headline, source-backed facts, useful internal links, metadata and a functioning next step.

On-page deliverables and acceptance

  • Page-to-query intent maps and cannibalization notes
  • Revised titles, descriptions, headings, and introductions
  • Internal-link recommendations with suggested source pages
  • Content gap and conversion-path notes
  • Before-and-after QA sheet for the updated URL

The on page seo handoff in practice

  1. Select priority URLs from search and conversion evidence
  2. Confirm one leading intent per URL
  3. Prepare edits with source and brand requirements
  4. Publish with approval and check live output

What we need from you

  • Target service and audience definitions
  • Access to current page and Search Console data
  • Approved brand language and compliance requirements
  • Publishing or developer approval contact

How to evaluate progress

Evaluate completion of approved page changes, search queries mapped to the page and the landing page’s engagement or enquiries. Do not infer a causal ranking lift from a single edit.

  • Non-branded query coverage by page
  • Impressions and qualified visits by intent
  • Engaged sessions or conversion events
  • Internal links from relevant pages

When not to create another landing page

Two near-identical pages targeting the same service in adjacent areas may split links and confuse visitors. Before writing a new URL, check whether one existing page can answer both groups with accurate service facts and clearer navigation.

Questions clients ask

Do we need new pages for every keyword?

No. Closely related queries usually belong on one strong page. A separate page makes sense when the user need and content are materially different.

Can you optimize existing pages first?

Yes. Existing pages may have links and search history worth preserving, making a careful refresh more efficient than replacing them.

Do you include conversion improvements?

We can identify headline, offer, trust, and CTA issues. Design and implementation work is scoped separately if needed.
