SEO Content Briefs for Australian Agencies

A keyword list is not a content brief. A strong Australian SEO brief explains who needs the page, which facts the business can substantiate, what the reader must decide and who should review sensitive claims.

Get a Quote →View sample deliverables

An Australian trades service page, a dental treatment page and a B2B software comparison should not come from one article template. Their readers have different concerns, the claims require different evidence and the approval process can vary from a branch manager to a registered practitioner or product owner.

Outsourced content work is most useful when the agency retains voice, source accuracy and publishing approval. The delivery output is a brief that saves the writer research time and saves the reviewer from rebuilding the argument from scratch.

First decide the page's job

A brief should state the search situation in a sentence: a homeowner checking emergency response and fees, a clinic patient asking about treatment suitability, a finance manager comparing software integrations. Group queries by the decision, not by superficial keyword variations. If two proposed pages answer the same decision with nearly identical proof, consolidate them.

Map the reader's next action before choosing an H1. Some pages need a booking CTA, others a quote form, a comparison table or simply a clear explanation. A content calendar that ignores the conversion path tends to generate traffic without useful enquiries.

Evidence that belongs in an Australian brief

For a plumbing company, gather actual service boundaries, licence details when relevant, call hours and what is included in a quote. For an Australian SaaS company, gather product documentation, integration limits, commercial-plan distinctions and tested screenshots. For a health provider, clinician approval and advertising-rule review are part of the work, not an optional editorial polish.

Australian English should be natural and consistent, but swapping 'optimization' for 'optimisation' does not make a page locally relevant. It needs accurate local terms, current rules where applicable, genuine operating details and sources the business can defend.

Worked example: a dental clinic's teeth whitening page

Illustrative example, not clinical advice. The keyword set includes teeth whitening by suburb, whitening price and suitability questions. A thin content brief might ask for 900 words with three headings and a stock smile photo.

A useful brief instead requests practitioner-approved treatment descriptions, who provides the service, how suitability is assessed, aftercare topics the clinic is authorised to explain, actual pricing presentation and what the initial consultation includes. The writer must not promise a specific colour change or insert patient praise about treatment outcomes as advertising.

The acceptance check is whether the practitioner has signed off the treatment and outcome wording, the booking path works, and the page answers the intended patient questions without relying on unsupported superlatives. The Ahpra rules are a review gate, not a marketing talking point.

How a brief prevents scaled-content problems

Do not generate 'dentist in Suburb A' and 'dentist in Suburb B' as separate pages if the only change is the suburb name. Document what is genuinely different about the clinic's service, location, staff, access or patient journey. When the proposed pages do not have distinct value, update one useful service page and improve navigation instead.

The brief should include a 'do not write' field for unsupported claims and a source list. A reviewer should be able to reject a page that is smooth but empty. Search keywords are inputs to the editorial decision, not permission to create unlimited URLs.

What a complete content handoff contains

Return a title direction, intent and audience, recommended outline, unique evidence requirements, internal links, CTA, prohibited or unverified claims, and acceptance criteria. If a writer has already drafted the page, include specific revision notes instead of a generic 'make it SEO friendly' comment.

For recurring engagements, track percentage of briefs approved first pass, research questions returned to the client, revisions caused by incorrect business facts and pages actually published. A brief is complete when the designated editor can commission or approve the page without reconstructing the research.

  • A concise reader decision and intent map.
  • Evidence requests separated from verified facts.
  • Page-level outline and non-negotiable answers.
  • A claim review table where Australian rules may apply.
  • Internal linking and primary action tested in the draft.

Questions agencies ask before delegating this work

Does an SEO brief include the finished article?

Not automatically. The proposal should distinguish keyword research, brief production, drafting, editing, subject-matter review and publishing.

Can one brief be reused for multiple Australian cities?

Only when those pages genuinely have different user needs and facts. A different suburb name alone does not justify a separate page.

Who approves regulated content?

The client’s designated qualified reviewer. Outsourcing content production does not transfer responsibility for health, legal, financial or other regulated claims.
