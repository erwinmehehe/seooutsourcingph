Our delivery workflow

Clear Ownership from Brief to Final Handoff

Every outsourced task needs an owner, an acceptance check and an approval path. Here is how that handoff can be structured.

A delivery system

Every step leaves something you can review.

The exact workflow is tailored to your organization and platform.

STEP 01

Discovery and baseline

Document the website, goals, access, current performance, stakeholders, and constraints. Unavailable data or access is visible from the start.

STEP 02

Scope and acceptance

Choose the first workstream, define outputs, identify reviewers, and write acceptance criteria for the work that will be handed back.

STEP 03

Production and QA

Specialists complete approved tasks, attach relevant evidence, test their work, and flag dependencies. An agreed reviewer checks the result.

STEP 04

Approval and release

The client or agency approves publishing when required. Tasks record what is live, what is still a recommendation, and the verification date.

STEP 05

Reporting and improvement

Separate completed work, observed search indicators, blocked items, and next actions. Adjust the next queue from evidence, not unverified promises.

What an approved SEO ticket contains

A task should carry the page or profile, priority reason, source evidence, expected output, responsible role, and review test. Technical changes may need an implementation owner and re-crawl; content changes may need brand and subject-matter signoff.

How white-label responsibility is different

For agencies, client contact and final delivery generally remain with the agency. The SEO team should not change client commitments or contact the end client without permission. Direct businesses can choose a simpler approval loop while keeping their own business facts and sensitive decisions.

What we measure

Delivery measures include accepted work and blocked dependencies. Search measures include dated ranking positions, Search Console trends, landing-page performance, and conversions where measurement is reliable. A single movement should not be claimed as a guaranteed result of one task.

Acceptance checklist for one SEO task

A task is ready for review when the other person can independently confirm what changed and why.

Buyer or scenarioRecommended pathReview standard
RequestSpecified target URL, outcome, evidence and exclusionsBrief approved
Work in progressOwner, blockers, source files and status visibleNo silent scope change
ReviewA reviewer checks source accuracy and technical behaviorAccepted, returned or blocked
Release and retestPublisher is known and a validation record is attachedChange can be independently verified

A “complete” status without evidence or responsible reviewer is not the same as accepted, published SEO work.

Approval responsibilities

One work item, four roles

A concrete approval model that can be adapted to the client’s process.

RoleDecision or responsibilityVisible handoff
Business / agency leadDefines the objective and approves the commercial scopeApproved brief and market facts
SEO delivery ownerPrepares work and supporting evidenceVersioned draft, issue list or implementation ticket
QA reviewerChecks accuracy, links, acceptance criteria and blockersAccepted, returned or blocked decision with rationale
Authorized publisherImplements approved changes and records validationPublished URL, change log and retest proof

White-label work should not put the outsourcing team in direct contact with an agency’s end client unless that boundary is approved. A useful weekly check-in highlights completed work, blocked tasks, decisions needed and next priorities instead of only reporting hours.
