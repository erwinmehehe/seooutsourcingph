From scope to completed work

The exact workflow is tailored to your organization and platform.

Discovery and baseline

Document the website, goals, access, current performance, stakeholders, and constraints. Unavailable data or access is visible from the start.

Scope and acceptance

Choose the first service, define outputs, identify reviewers, and write acceptance criteria for the work that will be handed back.

Production and QA

Specialists complete approved tasks, attach relevant evidence, test their work, and flag dependencies. An agreed reviewer checks the result.

Approval and release

The client or agency approves publishing when required. Tasks record what is live, what is still a recommendation, and the verification date.

Reporting and improvement

Review completed work, search performance and outstanding tasks, then agree the next priorities.

What an approved SEO ticket contains

A task should carry the page or profile, priority reason, source evidence, expected output, responsible role, and review test. Technical changes may need an implementation owner and re-crawl; content changes may need brand and subject-matter signoff.

How white-label responsibility is different

For agencies, client contact and final delivery generally remain with the agency. The SEO team should not change client commitments or contact the end client without permission. Direct businesses can choose a simpler approval loop while keeping their own business facts and sensitive decisions.

What we measure

Delivery measures include accepted work and blocked dependencies. Search measures include dated ranking positions, Search Console trends, landing-page performance, and conversions where measurement is reliable. A single movement should not be claimed as a guaranteed result of one task.

Reviewable work

  • Brief and scope
  • Work queue and owner
  • Technical or editorial evidence
  • QA and approval record
  • Monthly SEO report
